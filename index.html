Rules

  • A standard 52 card deck is used, with 2 to 5 players.
  • The number of cards dealt changes every round. It starts high, goes down one card at a time to a single card, then climbs back up again.
  • Trumps
    After the deal the trump coin is flipped. It has five faces: one for each suit and a blank. A blank means the round is played with no trumps.
  • Bidding
    Everyone looks at their cards and secretly bids exactly how many tricks they think they will win, placing a numbered token face down. All the tokens are revealed at the same time. Zero is a perfectly good bid.
  • Playing
    The player to the dealer's left leads the first trick. You must follow suit if you can. If you cannot, you may play any card. The highest card of the suit led wins the trick, unless a trump is played, in which case the highest trump wins. The winner leads the next trick.
  • Scoring
    You score 1 point for every trick you win, plus a 10 point bonus if you win exactly the number of tricks you bid.
  • The deal passes to the left each round. Highest total at the end wins.

Options

  • Bidding: In turn (dealer's hook)
    The traditional way. Players bid one at a time from the dealer's left, and the dealer, bidding last, may not make the bids add up to the number of tricks.
  • Trumps: Turned-up card
    The traditional way. The next card after the deal is turned face up and its suit is trump. There are no no-trump rounds.

Extended deck

  • Eight special cards are added: 2 Jokers 🃏, 4 Dragons 🐉, the Sun ☀️ and the Moon 🌙. A special card may be played at any time, even if you could follow suit. The lead suit is set by the first normal card played.
  • Who wins the trick
    1. If the Moon and the Sun are both played, the Moon wins, and its player scores a +20 capture bonus.
    2. Otherwise the Sun wins.
    3. Otherwise the first Dragon played wins.
    4. Otherwise the highest trump wins.
    5. Otherwise the highest card of the lead suit wins.
    6. If only Jokers and the Moon were played, the first card wins.
  • Jokers (and the Moon, when the Sun is not played) are perfect cards for losing a trick.
  • With the turned-up card option, a special card turned up means no trump.

Tips

  • The 10 point bonus is everything. Taking one trick too many usually costs you far more than it earns.
  • Once you have made your bid, get rid of your high cards when it is safe to do so: discard them when you cannot follow suit, or play them under a card that is already winning.
  • Watch the other players' bids. If the table has bid more tricks than there are, expect a fight for every trick.
  • When it is your turn your name will glow and cards you cannot play are dimmed.
